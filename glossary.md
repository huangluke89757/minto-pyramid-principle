# Glossary: Key Terms

## A
**Answer** (in SCQA): Your main point that resolves the Complication and answers the Question

**Argument**: A claim supported by evidence (distinct from dispute)

## C
**Complication** (in SCQA): The new event or problem that creates need for your Answer

**Conclusion**: The summary statement at the top of the pyramid (same as main point)

**Collective Exhaustiveness**: Grouping where all possibilities are covered; nothing is omitted

## D
**Deduction**: Logical reasoning from general principle to specific case (all X are Y, Z is X, therefore Z is Y)

**Desired Result (R2)**: The target outcome or goal state you want to achieve (Ch8)

**Diagnostic Framework**: A structured approach to problem-solving that guides analysis systematically (Ch9)

**Diagnostic Tree**: Visual representation showing all possible causes of a problem (Ch9)

## E
**Evidence**: Facts, data, examples that support a claim

## G
**Grouping Principle**: The shared characteristic that connects items in a horizontal level

## H
**Hierarchical**: Organized in levels with clear parent-child relationships

**Horizontal Relationship**: Connection between ideas at the same pyramid level (must be mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive)

## I
**Induction**: Logical reasoning from specific examples to general principle

## L
**Logic Tree**: Hierarchical diagram showing breakdown of a question into component parts

**Logical Order**: The sequence of ideas (chronological, structural, degree-based, or deductive)

## M
**Main Point**: The central idea at the apex of the pyramid

**MECE Principle**: Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive — framework categories must not overlap and must cover all possibilities (Ch9)

**Mental Imagery**: Using visual thinking and diagrams to clarify complex ideas (Ch12)

**Mutually Exclusive**: Grouping where items don't overlap; no idea belongs in multiple categories

## P
**Parallel Structure**: Using matching grammatical forms across headings/points at same hierarchy level (Ch10)

**Preamble**: The opening section (introduction or context) that frames the main point using SCQA

**Problem Definition Framework**: Structure for analyzing problems using Background-Complication-R1-R2 elements (Ch8)

**Pyramid Structure**: Hierarchical organization of ideas with one central idea supported by grouped reasons

## Q
**Question** (in SCQA): The issue raised by the Complication that your document will Answer

**Question-Answer Dialogue**: Vertical movement through pyramid where each level answers questions raised by the level above

## R
**Recommendation Pyramid**: A pyramid with actionable conclusion (e.g., "We should do X")

## S
**SCQA** (Situation-Complication-Question-Answer): Narrative framework for preambles

**Situation** (in SCQA): The context and background that reader already understands

**Supporting Idea**: Any idea below the main point that provides reasoning or evidence

## T
**Top-Down Thinking**: Expression method where main point comes first

**Topic Index**: Map of all topics and their corresponding chapters/sections

## U
**Undesired Result (R1)**: The current problem state; the gap between what is and what should be (Ch8)

## V
**Vertical Relationship**: Connection between pyramid levels where upper level raises questions answered by lower level

**Visual Hierarchy**: Using formatting, indentation, and sizing to show pyramid levels on a page (Ch10)

## W
**Why Test**: Asking "Why is this true?" to verify logical connection between levels

## Y
**Yes/No Question Analysis**: Binary decision trees used in diagnostic analysis to narrow down causes (Ch9)
