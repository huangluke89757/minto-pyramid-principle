# Minto Pyramid Principle

The Pyramid Principle is a rigorous method for organizing and expressing ideas using a hierarchical structure that mirrors how people naturally process information.

## Core Philosophy

**Think vertically and horizontally.** Ideas organized in a pyramid:
- Vertical axis: Question-answer dialogue that drives reader engagement
- Horizontal axis: Logical grouping using deduction or induction
- Top-down presentation: Conclusions first, supporting details follow
- Bottom-up thinking: Collect ideas, then summarize upward

## The Pyramid Principle (5 Core Frameworks)

### 1. The Pyramid Structure
A triangular arrangement of ideas where:
- **Top**: Single central idea (thesis)
- **Middle**: 2-4 major supporting arguments
- **Bottom**: Evidence, details, and proof

**When to use:** Every document, presentation, or complex explanation

### 2. SCQA (Situation-Complication-Question-Answer)
A narrative framework for preambles:
- **Situation**: Context the reader already knows
- **Complication**: Unexpected event or problem
- **Question**: The issue the reader now faces
- **Answer**: Your thesis/main point

**When to use:** Openings of reports, proposals, presentations

### 3. Vertical Logic (Induction & Deduction)

**Deductive Logic** (General → Specific):
- Major premise: All humans are mortal
- Minor premise: Socrates is human
- Conclusion: Socrates is mortal

**Inductive Logic** (Specific → General):
- Observation 1: Company A cut costs, profit rose
- Observation 2: Company B cut costs, profit rose
- Observation 3: Company C cut costs, profit rose
- Conclusion: Cost-cutting improves profitability

### 4. Horizontal Logic (Grouping & Ordering)
All items at one level must be:
- **Mutually exclusive** (no overlap)
- **Collectively exhaustive** (nothing missing)
- **Logically similar** (grouped by common characteristic)

**Ordering choices:**
- Deductive: 1→2→3 logically follows
- Inductive: Items share theme (reasons, problems, solutions)
- Chronological: Timeline order (first, then, finally)
- Structural: Spatial or organizational order (top to bottom)
- Degree: Importance or severity (high→low or vice versa)

### 5. The Question-Answer Dialogue

Each statement raises a question in the reader's mind. You must answer it at the next level down.

## Key Principles (Decision Rules)

1. **Conclusions First**: State your recommendation/finding before justification
2. **Top-Down Expression**: Descend the pyramid progressively
3. **Bottom-Up Thinking**: Collect ideas, then organize into pyramid
4. **One Idea Per Box**: Each pyramid level contains single thought
5. **Vertical Coherence**: Each level answers questions from above
6. **Horizontal Coherence**: Items at one level share logical relationship
7. **Logical Sufficiency**: 2-4 items per level; more indicates grouping needed
8. **Avoid "Laundry Lists"**: Never put miscellaneous items side-by-side

## Quick Reference: When to Use Pyramid

| Scenario | Approach |
|----------|----------|
| Writing a report | Use SCQA preamble + pyramid structure |
| Giving a presentation | Pyramid with visual hierarchy (one idea per slide) |
| Email to boss | SCQA structure: brief context, problem, question, answer |
| Analyzing a problem | Diagnostic framework + logic tree (Ch8-9) |
| Team communication | Agree on pyramid structure first, then details |
| Decision-making | Use question-answer dialogue to surface uncertainties |

## Anti-Patterns (What NOT to Do)

- Do NOT bury the main point at the end ("climax structure")
- Do NOT list disconnected ideas without logical links
- Do NOT make readers guess the hidden logic
- Do NOT provide evidence before raising the question
- Do NOT mix different types of grouping
- Do NOT use more than 4-5 items at any level without further grouping
- Do NOT assume readers can infer the connection between ideas
